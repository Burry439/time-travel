City Beyond Logic Map

How The City Beyond decides what happens: what the player does, who judges it, who may change the world, and how people come to know things.

Models propose. Only code changes the world.

Current as of 11 Oct 2026 · main · decisions up to D-73 (one source of truth; deals settled by quests)

A model proposes or judges Code decides and commits The player, or no change

The big picture

  • The client (Godot) moves the player and draws the world. It never decides outcomes; it sends requests and shows the state that comes back.
  • A backend command takes the journey's lock, checks the request, and asks the models outside the lock. Speech has a 12-second model budget, deeds 15 seconds.
  • Models only propose: the judge (judge_v28) for deeds and physics, each person's own call (person_v8) for what they say and choose, and the decider (OpenAI's Decisions API, D-71) for quick typed questions: is a typed line words or a deed, and would someone not addressed cut in.
  • The reducer checks every proposal against the world and the rules, then commits it as an event (consequences.py, rounds.py, talk.py). Journeys replay exactly from store.py.
  • The clock runs between your actions: routines, errands, fights, and stories checking whether something new has happened (clock.py, stories.py).

When you talk to someone

  • Each person is their own agent. Their call holds only their own sheet, memories, secrets and the rules that apply to them. Nobody's call contains anyone else's secrets.
  • Nobody cuts in unless it's their business (D-71). Before their turn, each person not addressed gets a quick check of their own (about 0.3 s): would they speak up now, and does it give them reason to act? Only someone who would speak up gets a turn that can speak; someone with reason to act (a guard overhearing a confessed theft) thinks it over in silence; anyone else carries on. Measured: interruptions in quiet scenes 8 of 57 rounds before, 0 after.
  • Code builds the moves a person may choose: answer, share a fact, confide a secret, agree to a story move (Darin letting a debt go), lend, give, send you to someone, let you through the gate. The person picks one, or none.
  • Words must match the move, and words stand. A yes to using their thing is a lend. Doing what they asked completes their deal. If code refuses part of what someone decided, their words stay and a plain note says what didn't happen (D-72). A guard who reads you as a threat lets you in only if they're afraid (duress). Promises to go and tell someone become real errands. A recommendation carries the request its speaker heard; the recipient responds when it arrives and may decide entry then.
  • The told reading runs beside the conversation and says whether your words passed on someone's secret. Whoever heard it, or overheard it, now knows it. A secret is never news to the person it's about (known_by).

When you do something

  • No recipes. The judge reasons about your method and what's actually at hand. New inventions keep their properties as attributes (a "magnetized" nail) for later use. Changes to existing objects also appear in reveal and inspection cards.
  • The validator holds the judge to physics: reach, mass (no matter from nowhere; water at hand can go into a poultice and leaves the pot), materials (bronze can't be magnetised), and capacity (a gourd bowl holds what a vessel its weight could).
  • Work continues. Reachable materials are walked to automatically; a blocked proposal involving a walk gets one bounded review. Already held pickup prerequisites are skipped. Assemblies retain actual attached parts, their mass and materials; fixed or unavailable attachments still block.
  • The world's building blocks. Every change a deed makes is one of a registry of general blocks (effects.py), each with a law, a reducer and someone who notices it: any named fact on a thing or person, tying things together, a state of a body (soaked, tied up, blindfolded: what it stops, and when it wears off), eating, writing and marks (a forged paper is just writing), touch and gesture. They ride on any action, so a kiss, water over a guard or chalk on bark really happen. Phase 2 added moving things (drag, roll, lean, set on or under, put across a way so nobody walks through), open, close, hide, containers and mixtures (pour from pot to pot, drink, stir honey in, foul the trough, soak, seal), and damage, mending and smashing. Liquid is conserved, contents go where their container goes, and a thing propped on another falls when that's moved. Phase 3 added signals (a shout heard through walls, a mirror's flash seen far off: those who can't see you only know which way it came from) and disguise (people who don't see through it pin your deeds on someone else; anyone who has met you sees through it up close), and terrain (dig a pit, fill it in, build a barrier or a plank bridge, set a covered pit or a snare that catches whoever walks into it). Phase 4 added fire and time: food cooks on a fire, wet things dry, clay is fired hard, smoke is seen far off, and anything left to take time (glue setting, clay drying) finishes on the clock if it's left as it needs, or stops if it isn't. Fire spreads to anything combustible lying beside it. That's the whole planned vocabulary: 39 kinds of building block, and the gap log shows what real play needs next.
  • Every deed can be seen. Whatever no specific rule covers is still seen as it looked; the person it touched remembers it and decides what it means. Words typed as a deed go to talk. What the world still can't do is listed by tools/gap_report.py from real play.
  • Witnesses are physical. Only people with real range and line of sight remember a deed firsthand; naming someone far away doesn't make them a witness. Repeating the same proof earns no new credit.

Who decides what

Fighting and equipment

The client shows anticipation immediately. The backend decides every hit from current gear, direction, reach and obstruction, on a 100 ms active clock. Each hand has its own recovery and a short input buffer. Weapons stay attached to articulated wrists; NPCs show their actual kit. The pack reads backend profiles, including damage and heavy two-hand handling.

Opponents position and defend deterministically; their movement can spring world traps. Their own model still chooses why to fight, flee, help or stop after significant changes. Shields guard toward your facing; a newly raised shield can parry. Walking remains evasion; no stamina or dodge key was added.

The village quests

Since D-70 the village's troubles are quests: five or more steps each, every step reachable several ways, and intertwined (one deed can move two quests, and doing one shows you its people's side of another). A step is a result in the world, checked by code; any way you find counts. Each step reaches a person when they come to know it, with their side of it and what they'd now ask of someone. People bring their trouble up themselves when you walk up to them. All eight are quests now, each played live (evals/run_quest_web_live.py).
