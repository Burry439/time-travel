# The City Beyond: a walkthrough, and fourteen things you can make

You come from the future. Nobody here knows what a compass, a siphon or a water filter is, and that is your advantage. This guide gives you one reliable way through the gate, then a catalogue of inventions that most people would never think to try, and who each one moves.

Every invention here was tried against the game's real judge (the model that decides what physically happens). **Tested** says how often it worked. There are no dice: when something fails, it's because the judge found a physical problem, and rewording to fix that problem usually works.

---

## How to act

- **One line does everything.** Type what you do or say. Quotes mean speech: `"Where's the gate?"`. Anything else is a deed.
- **Chain steps in one sentence.** "Make X, then show Y to Hessa" is one attempt. Each step can use what the one before it made.
- **Say how, not just what.** "Stroke the needle along the lodestone in one direction, float it on a leaf in the bowl" works. "Make a compass" gets a question back.
- **Show, don't tell.** People believe what they see. Claims move nobody much; a demonstration in front of them does.
- **Mouse:** left-click a person to talk, and a thing to pick it up (red means it's someone else's: stealing). Right-click inspects. Drag from your pack onto a person or thing to write the line for you.
- **E** acts on whatever is nearest. **Space** punches. **J** opens the journal. **T** or **Enter** opens the dialogue bar.

## The map in one breath

You start at the south end of the road. Going north:

1. **Hessa's inn** with its yard, trough and cooking fire, up the road and off to the west.
2. **Kade** patrols the middle of the road. **Elin** watches the road below the gate.
3. **The gate** is at the top, with **Roderic**, **Bren** and **Oren**, a water trough and some empty crates.
4. **Tomas's yard** is north-east of the middle road. **Darin's stall** is far east, and **Mara's forge** (with **Jory**) is far west.

Ask anyone "which way is the smithy?" and they'll tell you.

## What to pick up first (free, nobody owns it)

**Right around the start:** flint shard, short cord, length of vine, hollow reeds, two fallen branches, dry grass, pine resin, strip of bark, loose stone, flat stone, hazel rod, goose feathers, tuft of wool, wet clay, pine cones.

**Near the inn:** dried gourd (it holds water), old bone, broken pot shard.

**Up the road:** bundle of straw, scrap of leather, rusty horseshoe, burdock leaves (east), sheet of birch bark (far east), chalk (near Tomas), bent nail and a lump of charcoal (near the forge).

Things that belong to someone (Hessa's bowl, Mara's needles, Darin's lodestone, Tomas's rope) are theirs. **Ask to borrow them.** People lend what they're willing to lend, and they give things away once they trust you. Taking them is theft: the owner shouts, a guard comes about it, and walking off from a guard who came about a crime means a chase and an arrest.

## A reliable route in: the helper

This one route has been checked end to end: the deals, then the captain.

1. **Hessa (the inn).** Talk to her and offer to help. She asks you to bring a container full of water for her kitchen.
   - Fill your gourd at her trough and give it to her ("give Hessa the gourd of water").
   - She's satisfied and walks to the captain to speak for you.
   - For style, fill it with the **siphon** (below) and she'll talk about it.
2. **Mara (the forge).** She needs forge fuel. The lump of charcoal on her own ash heap, or dry branches and pine cones, will do. Bring some and she'll speak for you too.
   - Now she likes you. Ask to **borrow a needle**: you'll want it for the compass.
3. **Roderic (the gate).** Once someone he trusts has spoken for you, ask him to let you in. He weighs vouches and honest business, and never admits someone he knows has committed violence.
4. **Or Bren.** He lets people through on trust alone, or on his sister Hessa's word (tested: he let the stranger through on Hessa's word within two lines, every time).

## Other routes, built on inventions

- **The fog compass → Elin.** The watch keeps losing its way on the moor in fog, and Roderic and Elin both want a fix. Show Elin the compass (below) and ask to go through. Tested: she lets you through, or sends you to Roderic to show him.
  - **Don't show Roderic the compass cold.** He reads it as witchcraft and refuses you outright. Let Elin's report, or a vouch, do the work with him.
- **The merchant → Darin.** Show Darin something new he could sell (the speaking horn, the filter, the compass). If he likes it, he hires you under his trade permit and tells the captain. Roderic admits honest business.
- **The engineer → Tomas.** Tomas must get the cracked bar off the gate and the new 60 kg beam up into the brackets. Take the job, then:
  - lower the old bar (see "Lift the bar off", below);
  - raise the new beam **with Tomas helping** (below). He'll speak for you.
  - **Kade** respects only work he's seen done, so do it where he can see.
- **The wonder-worker → Oren and Jory.** Uncanny tricks (the obedient needle, climbing water) awe the superstitious. Jory spreads the word. Oren, awed, may open the gate himself. He's anxious and sometimes hands the decision to the captain instead (about 1–2 times in 3), so ask twice.
  - **Keep the tricks away from Roderic, Mara, Elin and Kade.** They see sorcery, and it makes them suspicious and afraid of you.
- **The hard way: the bar.** Lift the cracked bar off the gate and the gate stands open. But any guard within about 8 steps of the gate steps into the gap, and a guard who sees you do it treats it as an attack on the gate: a fight to the death, and the captain will never let you in afterwards. It only works if the guards are dead, down or elsewhere. Nothing in the game lures them away yet.

## Fourteen things you can make

The words in *italics* are lines that worked. You don't need them word for word; they show how much to say. "Wonder" is how it strikes a local.

### 1. The fog compass (uncanny)

**Need:** one of Mara's iron needles, Darin's lodestone, Hessa's bowl with water, a burdock leaf. **Show:** Elin, Darin or Jory.

*"Stroke a needle along the lodestone many times in one direction, float it on a burdock leaf in the bowl of water, and show Elin that it always swings round to point the same way, so a patrol could hold its heading in fog."*

**Tested:** worked 2/2 and 3/3, judged uncanny every time. Compass navigation is unknown here.

### 2. The obedient needle (uncanny)

**Need:** the compass set-up, with the lodestone hidden in your fist. **Show:** Oren or Jory, never Roderic.

*"Float a needle on a burdock leaf in the bowl of water, then with the lodestone hidden in my closed fist, pass my hand around the bowl so the needle turns to follow my hand as if it obeys me."*

**Tested:** worked 2/2 (uncanny once, clever once). This is how you make someone believe you're touched by the gods.

### 3. The siphon (uncanny)

**Need:** a hollow reed and your gourd, at the gate trough. **Show:** anyone, or just fill Hessa's order with it.

*"Fill one hollow reed completely with trough water, thumb over the top end, dip the other end deep in the gate trough, then bring the thumbed end down low against the ground beside the trough, well below the water line, into the mouth of the gourd, and let go."*

**Tested:** worked 2/2. The trick is the outlet: it must be **below the trough's water line**, or nothing flows.

### 4. The charcoal water filter (clever)

**Need:** gourd, flint, wool, the charcoal lump, dry grass, a bowl. **Show:** Hessa.

*"Cut the bottom off the gourd with the flint, pack it with a plug of wool, then crushed charcoal, then dry grass, and pour muddy trough water through it into the bowl to show Hessa how it comes out clear."*

**Tested:** worked 2/2. The judge is honest: it clears mud, but it doesn't make water safe. For that, **boil it** at Hessa's cooking fire ("boil the water in the bowl at the cooking fire"). Boiling water for safety is future knowledge here.

### 5. The mirror signal (clever)

**Need:** Darin's polished bronze mirror, borrowed. **Show:** Elin.

*"Catch the sun in the bronze mirror and flash it at the gate in long and short bursts, showing Elin how a patrol out on the moor could signal the watch from far away."*

**Tested:** worked 2/2. Mirrors are known here; coded flashing isn't.

### 6. The pendulum watch-timer (clever)

**Need:** the short cord and the loose stone. **Show:** Jory, or a guard who keeps the night watch.

*"Tie the stone to the cord and let it swing in small arcs, and show Jory that small swings of the same cord always take the same time, so counting swings keeps time."*

**Tested:** worked 2/2. Say **small** swings: the judge knows wide swings take a little longer.

### 7. The rope square (clever)

**Need:** Tomas's rope, the chalk, two stones. **Show:** Tomas, who loves measurements.

*"Tie knots in the rope marking twelve equal lengths, lay it on the ground as a triangle with sides of three, four and five lengths, pull it taut and pin two corners with the stones while I hold the third, and show Tomas that the corner between the three and four sides is a true square corner."*

**Tested:** worked 2/2. Without the stones to pin the corners, it's refused ("there are no pegs").

### 8. The bark speaking horn (clever)

**Need:** the sheet of birch bark and the cord. **Show:** Kade or Darin.

*"Roll the birch bark into a long cone and tie it with the cord, then shout through the narrow end to show Kade how much further a voice carries."*

**Tested:** worked 2/2.

### 9. Climbing water (sometimes uncanny)

**Need:** the wool, Hessa's bowl with water, her cup set a little lower. **Show:** Jory.

*"Twist the wool into a wick, lay one end in the bowl of water and the other end in the empty cup set a little lower, and show Jory how the water climbs up the wool and over the rim into the cup all by itself."*

**Tested:** it works, but it's slow: once uncanny, once ordinary, because greasy raw wool wicks badly.

### 10. The knee brace for Kade (kind)

**Need:** the hazel rod, the leather, the vine (not the short cord: too short), the flint. **Give to:** Kade.

*"Split the hazel rod into two splints with the flint, pad them with the leather, and bind them either side of a knee with the vine to make a brace, then give it to Kade for his bad knees."*

**Tested:** worked 2/2. Kade hides his failing knees from everyone. Noticing, and being kind about it, is the one way past his gruffness.

### 11. The resin torch

**Need:** a branch, the straw, the pine resin, the cord. **Where:** Hessa's cooking fire.

*"Bind the straw round the end of the branch with the cord, work the pine resin into it, and light it at the cooking fire to make a torch."*

**Tested:** worked 2/2. It burns down over time.

### 12. Fire from nothing but a stone and a horseshoe

**Need:** the flint, the rusty horseshoe, dry grass, a branch.

*"Strike the flint against the horseshoe to throw sparks into the dry grass, blow it into flame, and feed it the branch to make a small campfire."*

**Tested:** part of the regular checks (about 9 times in 10). Iron plus flint plus dry tinder; a missing piece fails. Once you have fire, you can heat water anywhere.

### 13. Lift the bar off the gate

**Need:** Tomas's ladder and rope, or the gate crates to stand on.

*"Set the ladder against the gate, loop the rope over the bar and lower it down to the ground, one end at a time."*

**Tested:** worked 3/3. By hand from the ground, it fails every time. A branch twisted into the rope as a **windlass** also works, about half the time. Mind the guards (see "The hard way").

### 14. Raising the new gate beam, with Tomas

**Need:** the old bar already down. Tomas's ladder and rope, the gate crates and the trestles.

*"With Tomas helping, set the trestles under the brackets and rest the new beam on them, then Tomas and I each stand on a crate at one end, heave our end up onto our shoulder and push it up into its bracket, one end at a time, with the rope tied to the other end so it can't slip."*

**Tested:** worked 2/2. Levering it up in stages alone works only about half the time: trestles and crates don't reach two metres.

## Who reacts how to wonders

| Person | Wonders make them… | Lets you through when… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roderic (captain) | suspicious: witchcraft | someone he trusts vouches, you have honest business, or you've shown him something of real value (not a trick) |
| Bren (guard) | curious | he trusts you himself, or Hessa has spoken for you |
| Oren (guard) | awed, even afraid of you | he's in awe or afraid of you, or his debt to Darin is settled |
| Elin (scout) | uneasy, but she values what works | you've shown something that helps the patrols in fog, or to see danger coming |
| Kade (guard) | unmoved: tricks get men killed | he's seen your real, practical work with his own eyes |
| Hessa (inn) | gently moved | (not a guard) she speaks for you after you help her |
| Mara (smith) | wary: sorcery near her forge and nephew | (not a guard) she speaks for you for forge fuel |
| Jory (apprentice) | thrilled, and tells everyone | (not a guard) |
| Tomas (carpenter) | curious how it works | (not a guard) he speaks for you once the beam is up |
| Darin (merchant) | sees a trick worth money | (not a guard) he hires you for something he can sell |

## A few last things

- **People keep their purpose.** If Kade comes about Tomas's rope, an apology won't make him forget it. Hand it back, and it's over.
- **Walking off** from a guard who came about a crime means a chase. They'll try to put you on the ground and arrest you, not kill you, unless you've drawn blood or touched the gate bar.
- **Space** punches anyone within arm's reach, in a fight or starting one. Fists do nothing to a man in mail.
- **Boiling water** thrown in a face gets past any armour. The other guards will probably kill you for it.
- **The journal (J)** keeps what people told you, your deals, and what you've overheard.
