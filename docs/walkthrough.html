City Beyond Walkthrough

You arrive from the future with nothing but what you know. The city gate to the north is shut to strangers. Get inside, any way you can.

Starting a game

  1. From the project folder run .\tools\run_demo.ps1. It starts the backend and opens the game.
  2. Choose New journey. Saves from before today's changes can't be continued.
  3. You stand on the south road. Everything below is one way to play; any other way you can describe also counts.

Current as of 5 Oct 2026 · claude/quests · D-67 (first quests)

How it works

  • Win: someone with the right to let you in agrees (the captain, or any gate guard by their own rule), then you walk north through the gate.
  • Say and Do in plain words. There are no magic phrases. "Fill the gourd at the trough" and "dip the gourd in the water" both work. The lines below are examples, not passwords.
  • People decide for themselves. They remember what you did, tell each other, and can say no. If someone refuses, ask what would change their mind.
  • The world follows physics. The judge rules on whether a deed can work with what's at hand, and says why when it can't. Nearby materials are walked to automatically. Retrying can use what you already carry. Existing bindings stay in an assembly.
  • Things belong to people. Taking something that isn't yours shows "Steal" in red. Witnesses remember it.

The village

Ways into the city

Each guard has a rule of their own. Any one of them agreeing is enough. The first three are tested end to end; the rest follow each person's own rule.

Hessa vouches · easiest

Carry water for the innkeeper

  1. Pick up the dried gourd by the inn.
  2. Ask Hessa for work: "Can I help with anything?" She asks for water for her kitchen.
  3. Agree, fill the gourd at her trough (drag it onto the trough), and give it to her.
  4. She walks to the gate to speak for you. Wait until she has (her label shows where she's going).
  5. If you asked Hessa to help you get in, her recommendation carries that request. Roderic responds when she arrives; he may let you through or ask for a missing detail. Go to him if needed, then walk north once admitted.
Tested
Darin hires you

Show the merchant something new to sell

  1. At the start area, pick up the hollow reeds and the flint.
  2. "Cut a short length of reed with the flint and carve a notch to make a whistle."
  3. Offer Darin something new he could sell. Agree to his deal, then blow the whistle for him.
  4. He takes you on under his trade permit. Tell Roderic you're Darin's hired hand.
Tested
Tomas vouches

Raise the new gate beam

  1. Offer Tomas help with the beam; agree to his job.
  2. Borrow his rope and ladder (you can carry 20 kg, so not everything at once).
  3. At the gate, take the cracked bar down first, then raise the new beam into its brackets.
  4. Tell Tomas it's done. He speaks for you to the captain.
Tested
Elin, or the captain

A compass for the watch

  1. Ask Elin what's hardest about the watch: the fog.
  2. Borrow Darin's lodestone. Pick up the bent nail and stroke it on the stone. Give the stone back.
  3. Float the nail on bark in a bowl that isn't iron (cut the dried gourd into one); an iron pot pulls the needle off north.
  4. Show Roderic and Elin it points north, then give it to them.
Judge varies on a weak magnet
Mara vouches

Fuel for the forge

Mara asks for fuel. There's charcoal west of the smithy and a sack in the wainwright's yard.

Bren

A friend at the gate

Bren bends the rule for someone he trusts, or someone his sister Hessa has vouched for. Never for a stranger.

Oren

Awe, fear or his debt

Oren lets in someone he's in awe of, or someone who has settled his debt with Darin. See "Oren's debt" below.

Kade

Honest work, seen

Kade respects only practical work he has watched with his own eyes. Talk and tricks count for nothing.

You can also take the bar off the gate yourself. It's a crime, the guards will see it, and they will deal with you.

The village quests

People have troubles of their own, and when you walk up to them they'll often bring them up themselves. Each quest has five or more steps, each step can be done more than one way, and they cross: helping one person brings you back to another, and you'll hear their side of it. None is required to win. Your journal's Stories page shows every step you've reached, whose side it came from, and what people have told you, which you can pin.

The Wayside Inn (Hessa) Quest · played live

Starts
Walk up to Hessa: her guests keep falling sick from the trough.
Steps
What's wrong with the water; the cause (ask her, or Jory, who saw something dead); clean water (clean the trough, boil it, or teach her); the lodging money; what her brother's hiding; an old grief about Kade.
One way
Ask what changed at the trough, pry up the rotten boards with a stout stick, dig out the rot and bail it clean. She'll speak for you, and may confide that Darin owes her.

Dice and Debts (Oren) Quest · played live

Starts
Walk up to Bren: he'll ask you to find out what's eating Oren.
Steps
Something's wrong; the eleven silver (Oren, Darin or Jory can tell you); Darin's squeeze; Bren covering Oren's watches; the debt answered (forgiven, paid in goods, or Oren owns up to the captain).
One way
Jory saw the dice game. Show Darin something he can sell, learn his permit needs the captain's seal, and point out a squeezed guard sours the captain. He forgives it, and his own name in town comes clean.

The Merchant's Permit (Darin) Quest · partly played

Starts
Walk up to Darin: he wants something new to sell.
Steps
What he wants; something to sell; the permit running out; a clean name in town (his debts squared, or Hessa speaking for him); the captain's seal; hired.
Crosses
Oren's debt and Hessa's lodging money both count toward his clean name.

Worth trying

  • Win over a superstitious guard. Show Roderic or Mara the compass and they'll take it for witchcraft. Explain how it truly works and watch their fear turn to wonder. "It's a gift from the gods" won't do it.
  • Forge a pass. Write on bark with chalk and show it to a guard. It's just writing; they decide whether to believe it.
  • Do something to someone. Tip water over a guard, kiss someone, tie up someone who's down. It really happens, everyone nearby sees it, and the person decides how they feel. A conscious, standing person won't let you tie them.
  • Rearrange the world. Roll the log across the path and people walk around it. Lean the ladder on a wall, drag the crates under the gate brackets, hide a note under a stone.
  • Mix and pour. Stir honey into a bucket of water and drink it, or foul the trough. Water you pour goes somewhere: into another pot, over someone, or onto the ground. Seal a pot shut, and a broken seal shows.
  • Call for help, or signal from afar. A shout carries through walls; a flash of Darin's mirror is seen from the gate. People who can't see you only know which way it came from, and they decide whether to come.
  • Go in disguise. Pull a cloth over your head. People who've never met you take you for someone else, and what you do is pinned on that someone. Anyone who knows your face sees through it up close.
  • Dig and build. Dig a pit with a stick and a flat stone, cover it with branches and grass, and whoever walks into it is stuck for a while. Set a snare, stack crates into a barricade, or lay a long branch over a pit as a bridge.
  • Cook, dry and fire. Roast garlic on Hessa's fire and wait for it; dry a wet cloth by the flames; fire a clay cup hard in the hearth so it holds water. Leave clay to dry and it sets hard in time, if nobody moves it. Straw left by the fire catches.
  • Break and mend. Hole a bucket and it leaks; mend it and it holds water again. Smash someone's bowl and it's their loss.
  • Wait. "Wait here an hour" lets the world go on around you: what's cooking cooks, what's drying dries.
  • Lie at the gate. Tell a guard Hessa vouched for you before she has. They check.
  • Pass on a secret. Tell someone what another person confided. Whoever hears it, or overhears it, now knows it, and the news travels.
  • Watch news travel. People walk over to tell each other what they saw. A label over their head shows where they're going.
  • Get hurt and treat it. Wounds bleed and fester. Make a bandage or poultice from what you find.
  • Ask the device. Press H when stuck. It coaches from what you know, never from secrets.

Rough edges to expect

  • Replies take about 4 to 8 seconds; a group at the gate can take up to 12.
  • People sometimes say no. That's their choice. Try another angle, or ask what would change their mind.
  • Pictures of new or changed things are painted in the background and can take a little while to appear. A carved grip or other change can keep the same item: its reveal and right-click card show its new attributes.
  • Heavy creations can be left at your feet when your pack is full. Cutting iron still needs a physically suitable method and tools.
  • If you die, you can rewind to the last calm moment.
  • Note anything confusing, unfair or slow, with what you typed. That's what the next round of fixes is for.
