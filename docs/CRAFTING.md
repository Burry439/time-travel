# Try Something

**New here?** Choose **Tutorial (first time)** on the title screen. "The first morning" takes about 20 minutes: the device that brought you here walks you through it one thing at a time: moving, looking closer, picking things up, your pack, talking, making a remedy, treating someone, a practice bout with Kade, and showing off an invention. Each step starts with big cards in the middle of the screen while the world waits; press **Next** when you've read them. Then one task shows at the top, and a glowing ring marks what it's about. Stuck? Press **H**. Where it gives you exact words, **Type it for me** fills them in. Afterwards, press **H** any time to ask the device for a nudge. It never gives away the answers, and you can switch it off in Settings.

**The mouse:**

- **Hover** over anything to see what it is and what a click does.
- **Left-click** to talk to someone or to pick something up. If it isn't yours, the label is red: that's stealing.
- **Right-click** to look closer.
- **Drag** things from your pack (bottom of the screen) onto people or things.

Press **T** (or Enter), or click something, and the dialogue bar opens with one line. Beside it, **SAY** / **DO** (Tab switches) says which you mean: SAY is words spoken aloud (it starts on SAY when you're talking to someone), DO is something you do with your hands (words in "quotes" in a DO line are said as well). A live preview under the line shows how it will read. "I wait", "I give up" and "I walk away" work in either. **E** acts on whatever the "[E] …" tag is over. **H** opens the device: ask it anything, and it remembers your whole conversation. **J** opens your journal on your goals: pin one to follow it, and the person it's about is marked in the world.

You can describe several things in one go ("sharpen the branch into a spear with the flint, then stab Tomas with it"). They happen in order, and if one fails the rest don't happen. The chips on the right point at things; clicking one is optional. There are no command words. The AI judge decides what happens, and code checks the result before anything changes.

## What you can do

- **Make** something new: "lash the flint to the branch with the vine to make a spear". The new object goes in your bag with its own history, and a picture is painted for it (a pulsing stand-in shows until it arrives).
- **Take** things, including other people's: "grab Tomas's plank". It works, but it is theft, and anyone who sees it may react. Things someone has **lent** you are fine to use.
- **Take part of something** bigger: "pull a leaf off the oak", "snap a twig off the sapling", "peel a strip of bark", "scoop a handful of the clay". You get a small new thing (at most 1 kg) made of what it came from; the tree stays standing, and a small lump gets that much smaller. Every big tree gives leaves, twigs, bark and resin.
- **Fill** a container with water: "fill the gourd at the trough". Water is conserved; the troughs are at Hessa's inn and by the gate.
- **Show** what something does: "float the needle in the bowl and show Darin". The judge says what onlookers actually see, and the object's abilities become *seen working* or *didn't work*. Showing beats describing: people remember what they saw.
- **Give** something you carry to someone: "give Hessa the gourd of water".
- **Raise or fit** something into place: "lever the gate beam up in stages and ease it into the brackets". Heavy things need real help (a lever and fulcrum, something to crib it up on, a pulley you make, a ladder), and the judge is honest about whether it works. If it works, the beam really stays up. A fixture holds one thing at a time.
- **Take something down** out of its fixture: "stand on the crates, lift one end of the bar out of its bracket, then the other, and let it down on the rope". The cracked bar across the city gate is what holds it shut: with it down, the gate stands open. The guards see that as an assault on the gate, and any guard near the gate will step into the gap. Doing it as Tomas's swap (his deal accepted) is just work.
- **Offer a trade**: "offer Darin my whistle for his lodestone". They answer when you next talk. People who trust you may also simply give you their things.
- **Cut, break or fell** something: "cut down the young birch with the flint shard".
  - The tree falls over and leaves pieces (trunk, branches, stump). Each gets its own picture. Anything up to 20 kg can be picked up.
  - A felled map tree no longer blocks the path.
  - Big oaks and cypresses need real tools and hours of work. The three saplings (by the road start, east of it, and near the craftsmen's row) are realistic with a flint.
- **Attack** someone within reach: "sneak up behind him and drive the stake into his neck". The judge rules what really happens, with no dice: missed, deflected by armour, wounded, disabled (down but alive) or killed, depending on the weapon, their armour and whether they saw you coming. A wooden stake glances off a guard's mail but kills an unarmoured man; boiling water in the face gets past any armour. A death is permanent: the body stays down and the name shows "(dead)"; someone disabled shows "(down)".
- **Then it's a fight.** Guards who see it come at you at once, in real time. Each blow has a wind-up (a pulsing red "!"); step out of reach before it lands and it misses. **Space** uses what's in your right hand and **F** your left (empty hands punch); hold **F** with a shield to raise it against blows from the front; **X** swaps hands; **Q** throws what's in your right hand. Your blows wind up too: a knife is quick, a sledge slow, a bow must be drawn. Press T and say you give up to be arrested instead.

## Weapons, armour and wounds

- **Anything you make is something**: a spear, an axe, a bow, a padded jacket, a shield, a bandage. Press **I** for **Pack & gear**: take things in either hand, put armour and clothes on, see what each is good for ("Deadly: stabs, reaches far, one hand").
- **What a blow does is physics.** An iron sword cuts an unarmoured man down in two blows; a fire-hardened wooden spear is dangerous bare, useless against mail; a rock is deadly to the head of someone who doesn't see it coming. Armour matters a great deal: mail turns almost any cut, but a heavy blow still bruises through it.
- **Say it and it can work**: "sneak up behind him and bash his head in with the stone", "cut his throat while his back is turned", "strangle him with the cord until he stops moving". Someone who sees you coming is much harder to catch, and a choke needs them unaware.
- **Wounds.** Cuts bleed until you bind them, burns and open wounds fester after a while, a hard blow can break an arm (no shield, no two-handed weapon) or a leg (no running). Ordinary hurts mend slowly on their own, but not while a wound is untreated. Make what you need: linen strips for a **bandage**, a needle and thread for **stitches**, sticks and cord for a **splint**, crushed plantain or yarrow (by the road), moss (by the wall), garlic or honey (Hessa's) for a **poultice**. Treat yourself in Pack & gear, or someone else by saying so; people remember who helped them.
- **Everyone's gear is real.** The guards' spears and mail, Roderic's sword: take them from someone down or dead (it's theft, and seen as such).
- **There's no fighting mode.** Space and F swing whenever you like, at someone or at thin air; the blow lands on whoever is in front of you when it lands. When people attack you, you can still talk (plead, explain, call for help, give up), treat a wound, or run.

Code always checks:

- You can't invent materials, and nothing weighs more than what went into it.
- Nothing burns or is magnetic without a real cause.
- People must be within reach, and a weapon must actually be at hand.
- Fists and light, soft things can't hurt anyone through mail, whatever the judge says.

## Materials in the world

- **The south road start:** branches, cord, stones, flint, flat stone, dry grass, reeds, vine, bark, resin, hazel rod, pine cones and a birch sapling.
- **The rest of the map:** feathers, wool, a bone, a gourd, wet clay, a pot shard, straw, leather, a **bent iron nail** and a **rusty horseshoe** (iron!), charcoal, chalk, bramble, burdock leaves, birch bark, a heavy log, and two more saplings.
- **The old wainwright's yard (bottom-right corner, free to take):** 48 materials for trying out weapons and armour. Metal: iron bars, a steel billet, a bronze ingot, a copper sheet, iron plates, nails, rivets, chain and wire, an unfinished knife blade, a sickle blade, a spearhead and an axe head. Stone and glass: flint, chert, obsidian, green glass, river stones for a sling, a whetstone and a hammerstone. Wood: ash and yew staves, an oak billet, elm planks, an axe haft, arrow shafts, willow withies and birch poles. Hide and fibre: rawhide, tanned leather, thongs, sinew, flax cord, hemp rope, linen, quilted padding and wool. Also antler, horn, bones, hide glue, pine pitch, beeswax, goose feathers and charcoal. And **the wainwright's notebook**: pick it up and click it in your pack to read 33 ways to make weapons, missiles, shields, armour, remedies and tools from these materials, each with words to try (**Type it for me**). The world still judges every attempt.
- **Everywhere:** all 19 big trees.
- **Owned by NPCs:** Hessa's bucket, bowl and cup; Tomas's plank, rope, ladder, fulcrum stone and trestles; Darin's **lodestone**, linen, cordage and bronze mirror; Mara's **iron needles** and nails.
- **At the gate:** the replacement **gate beam** (60 kg), the **iron brackets** it belongs in, some empty crates and a water trough.

To add your own, edit `data/content/world_objects.json`. Then run:

```powershell
backend/.venv/Scripts/python.exe tools/generate_material_art.py
```

Start a **new journey** to see new content; existing journeys keep their original world. Journeys from much older versions (before the materials across the map) can't be resumed.

## Talking and getting into the city

Your **journal's Leads** list what people have told you, where they've pointed you and what they've asked of you, and mark each one done when it is. When someone's view of you shifts, you'll see a small sign ("Darin looks more interested"), never numbers.

If the guards catch you, giving up ("I give up") is usually wiser than fighting: they take back anything you stole, march you down the south road, and keep you from the gate for a while. You can still get in later; only real violence closes the gate for good.

Click someone to talk. When someone comes up to *you* (a guard halting you, someone confronting or attacking you), the same bar opens by itself.

It works in **rounds**. Each round you type what you say or do ("hand him the plank", "swing the spear at her", or both at once), or wait, give up, or walk away (Esc). Then the people there answer what you just said and did. **It's a group:** the person you spoke to answers, and others in the conversation or standing close by may chime in, talk to each other, or ask you something. Anyone nearby who speaks steps over and joins. Showing someone something ("I show Roderic the compass") starts a conversation with them too.

Time only moves round by round while you're face to face. Walking away from a fight gives an attacker a swing, and they may chase you.

**People remember you.** Tell someone your name and they'll use it; they recall what you told them and what you did, and they hold a lie against you. They also talk to each other: news of what you did travels, and you can overhear it (it goes in your journal). Everyone has their own day, moods and opinions of each other; hover or right-click someone to see how they seem and what they're doing.

Type anything. The person reads the moment and answers in their own voice, and does one thing: answer, ask you something, tell you what they know, send you to someone, lend you something, offer a deal, speak for you, demand something back, forgive you, refuse, send you away, or fetch a guard. Guards arrest only for a real crime that hasn't been put right. People nearby overhear. Your journal lists what people told you and your deals.

- **Words alone move people only a little.** Showing them something that works, giving them something, or someone speaking for you counts for much more.
- **Deals:** some people will ask something of you (Tomas, Hessa, Darin, Mara) and, once they see it done, go and speak for you to the captain.
- **The gate:** guards stop strangers. Only Captain Roderic can let you in, when he believes you're no threat and have a real reason: someone he trusts speaks for you, you work for someone with honest business, or you've shown him something of real value. He won't after a crime he knows about.
- **Show something** by saying so ("float the needle in the bowl and show Darin"), or drag it onto them; then ask what they think.

## Set up the AI (once)

1. Put your key in **`backend/.env`**, never in `.env.example`, which is shared in git: `OPENAI_API_KEY=...`
2. Restart the backend from the project root:
   ```powershell
   ./tools/stop_m0.ps1; ./tools/run_m0.ps1
   ```
3. The title screen stops saying "AI crafting is off".

## Debugging what the model did

Start a **Developer playtest**.

- **Every result in the dialogue bar** has a *Details* link with the developer trace (in developer journeys the bar also says how your line was taken, e.g. "heard as do (player)" when you chose the mode):
  - The model, latency, tokens and any errors
  - What the model proposed vs. what the validator committed, with a note for every change
  - Buttons to show the raw model JSON, the exact context sent to it, and the committed object
- **F4** (or the F3 panel's *AI traces* button) lists the last 10 attempts, sprite jobs with their prompts and errors (with *Retry painting*), the model-call log, and the judge's full system prompt.
- The same data is available at `http://127.0.0.1:8765/runs/<run_id>/traces`.

## Tests

- **Backend:** `backend/.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m pytest backend/tests -q`
- **Godot flow, no key needed:**
  1. Start the backend with `$env:AI_MODE='stub'; ./tools/run_m0.ps1`.
  2. Run `Godot_v4.7.2-stable_win64_console.exe --headless --path . --script res://tools/smoke_ui.gd` (and `smoke_talk.gd`, `smoke_craft.gd`). Leave out `--headless` and add `-- --capture` to save screenshots to `docs/screenshots/`.
  3. Restart the backend normally afterwards.
- **Live judge quality** (costs about a cent per run): `backend/.venv/Scripts/python.exe evals/run_craft_evals.py --runs 3`. It runs 19 cases, including bad physics, ownership, felling, attacks and prompt injection, and writes a report to `evals/results/`.
- **Live conversations** (about a tenth of a cent per exchange): `evals/run_talk_evals.py --runs 2`: pleading, injection, rudeness, knowledge boundaries, deals, evidence and crimes at the gate.
- **Live memory, voices and village** (a few cents each): `evals/run_memory_evals.py` (do people remember you across conversations?), `evals/run_persona_evals.py [--baseline]` (can a judge tell who said each line?), `evals/run_world_live.py` (ten simulated minutes of the village on its own).
- **Live routes into the city** (a few cents): `evals/run_routes_live.py` plays the sponsor, business and value routes end to end and reports whether each got in.
